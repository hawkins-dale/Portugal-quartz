### Self-Guided Walk

**Porto Walk**: Two-part self-guided walk linking the city's top landmarks.

### Churches & Religious Sites

**Porto Cathedral**. Worth seeing. Fine _azulejo_-decorated cloister; otherwise dull interior.

**Clérigos Church and Tower**: Do the multimedia show, skip the tower — too crowded.

**Igreja de São Francisco**: Gothic church dripping with Baroque gold.

**Igreja do Carmo**: Spectacular blue tile facade depicting the founding of the Carmelite Order.

**Capela das Almas**: Covered in blue tile.

**Misericórdia Church and Museum**: Church connected to a former hospital, now home to a fine exhibit showing the good works and treasures of Porto's all-powerful charity.

### Museums & Culture

**MUST DO:**
**[[Serralves]] art museum / Foundation Contemporary Art Museum and Park.** Sprawling park with impressive museum, Art Deco mansion, and relaxing grounds.

**Museu Nacional Soares dos Reis**: Art museum.

**Photography Museum**: Free. Cool building. Photojournalism, contemporary photo art.

**Palacio da Bolsa**: Former stock exchange. Book online, but still go and wait for the next English tour — if waiting, walk around.

**Livrario Lello**: The famous bookstore. You **must** have tickets. Always _jammed_.

**World of Wine**: In Gaia. Museum of port, chocolate, and wine heritage. Includes cafés, restos, and shops. Views are cinematic.

### Views & Sunsets

**[[Miradouro da Serra do Pilar]]**: Walk across the Dom Luis I bridge and watch the sunset.

⭐️ **[[Jardim do Morro]]** at sunset.

**[[Miradouro da Vitória]]**

**Parque das Virtudes**: Good view in the afternoon.

**Foz do Douro**: The beach! 17th-century fortress. Sunsets.

### Neighborhoods & Streets

**Ribeira District** (UNESCO World Heritage site): Includes the Dom Luis I bridge, which is cool.

**Cais da Ribeira**: Porto's picturesque riverfront, with arcades and colorful traditional homes.

**Vila Nova de Gaia**: Explore the historic old city and riverside.

**Avenida dos Aliados**: Porto's top urban street, where the city goes to work in elaborate buildings.

**Rua de Santa Catarina**: Main shopping drag, with Art Nouveau and Art Deco landmarks. See also [[Shopping in Porto|Shopping]].

**Bonfim**: Neighborhood east of downtown. Non-touristy, working-class. Great market. 20-minute walk from the river.

See also [[Neighborhoods to Avoid]].

### Markets

**São Bento Train Station**: Entry hall decorated with impressive _azulejo_ (tile) murals. Pop-up market outside on weekends.

**Bolhão Market**: Lively produce and meat market, touristy but still a lot of great stuff. Lower level: market. Upper level: restos and sometimes art exhibits.

See also [[Porto Markets]].

See also [[Shopping in Porto]]

### Other Activities

**Six Bridges boat tour**: quite cool. [Book here](https://www.civitatis.com/en/porto/six-bridges-cruise/).

**River cruise**: Cheaper to depart from across the river on the Gaia side.

**O Porto dos Gatos** (the Cat Café): Has cats. Vegetarian food. Profits go to the cat pound.

### Weather

It **will rain**. Umbrellas are available from street vendors. If you have an umbrella, it's fun to walk around.

### Transport

- Porto is **dense**. Walk everywhere.
- [[Buses in Porto]] are excellent. Use https://stcp.pt/en/plan-a-trip
- The [[Metro in Porto]] is cool: most lines are mostly above ground.
- Or ride bikes! Lots of bike lanes. Or an [e-bike tour](https://www.bajabikes.eu/en/porto-highlights-tour/)!
- Bolt is better than Uber. It's the local (cheaper) version.
- Car rental isn't bad! [[portugal car rental]]
